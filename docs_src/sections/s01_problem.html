Problem Statement and Motivation

Small and mid-sized utility providers and housing-society utility offices in India still run electricity, water and piped-gas services with paper meter cards, spreadsheets and counter-based payment. Field staff write readings in registers, office staff re-type them, and bills are worked out by hand from tariff tables that change every year. This causes:

  • Billing errors – progressive (slab) tariffs are easy to calculate wrongly by hand; a single mistyped reading produces a wrong or even negative bill.
  • No single view of a customer – electricity, water and gas records live in different files, so the office cannot see a customer's total dues or history in one place.
  • Slow payments and long queues – customers must visit a counter, and payment status is updated late.
  • Poor complaint and outage handling – complaints are lost, and customers are not told about outages in their area.
  • No management information – managers cannot easily see revenue, pending dues, consumption by area or overdue bills.

Problem statement. Design and build a centralised, database-driven web application that manages customers, connections, meters, meter readings, slab-based billing, payments, complaints and outages for electricity, water and gas, with separate role-based views for administrators, employees and customers.

Motivation. The topic was assigned for our DBMS course project. Because it is a realistic multi-user system with clear business rules, we chose to build the same system for the SEPM course as well, following the Waterfall model. The DBMS course focuses on the schema, constraints, triggers and views; this SEPM report focuses on the engineering process: feasibility, requirements, design, implementation, testing and project management.
