Normalisation

  • 1NF: every column holds one atomic value; repeating data (many readings, many payments, many slabs) is in its own table, not in repeated columns.
  • 2NF: every table has a single-column surrogate primary key, so there are no partial dependencies. For natural composite keys such as (meter_id, reading_date), the non-key columns depend on the whole key.
  • 3NF / BCNF: there are no transitive dependencies. For example, tariff rates are not stored in connections; a connection points to a tariff plan and the plan points to its slabs. Utility units live only in utilities.
  • Deliberate denormalisation: bills stores units, energy charge, fixed charge, tax and total at the time the bill is issued. Tariffs change over time, but an issued bill is a legal record and must never change. meter_readings.consumption is also stored so that a reading row is self-explanatory and cannot drift.

Database objects that enforce business rules

Example – the overpayment trigger (from schema.sql):

CREATE TRIGGER trg_no_overpay
BEFORE INSERT ON payments
WHEN NEW.amount > (SELECT ROUND(total_amount - amount_paid, 2) FROM bills WHERE bill_id = NEW.bill_id) + 0.001
BEGIN
    SELECT RAISE(ABORT, 'Payment exceeds outstanding balance');
END;

Billing formula

For each slab i with range [fromi, toi) and rate ri (the last slab has no upper limit):

units in slabi = max(0, min(U, toi) − fromi)     Energy = Σ units in slabi × ri     Total = (Energy + Fixed) × (1 + Tax% / 100)

Worked example (domestic electricity, 250 kWh): 100 × 3.50 = 350.00; 150 × 5.00 = 750.00; energy = 1,100.00; + fixed 50.00 = 1,150.00; + 5% tax 57.50 = ₹1,207.50 (verified by test UT-01).
